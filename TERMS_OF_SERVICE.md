## 1. What Insina Health is

Insina Health is a personal health record application, currently in a
**pre-commercial pilot phase**. It helps you organize your own health
information — conditions, medications, labs, vitals, and more — and offers
AI-assisted analysis of that information.

**Insina Health is not a medical device, and it does not diagnose, treat, or
direct medical care.** Every AI feature is informational only: it presents
your own data back to you, and suggests questions to raise with your care
team. It never tells you what you have, what to do about it, or that a
symptom or lab value should be treated as an emergency on its own authority —
see the AI-limitations notice you're shown before first use.

Nothing in this app or these terms is a substitute for professional medical
advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Always consult a qualified clinician with
questions about your health, and call emergency services for anything that
may be a medical emergency.

## 3. Your account and your data

Insina Health has a **non-custodial** architecture: your health record is
stored on your own device, encrypted under a passphrase only you know, with
no server-side copy. This means:

- **You are responsible for your passphrase and recovery key.** There is no
  password reset. If you lose both your passphrase and your one-time recovery
  key, your data cannot be recovered by Insina Health or anyone else — the
  encryption is designed so that this is true.
- **You are responsible for backing up your data** if you want a copy outside
  this device (via the export feature or Google Drive sync).
- You retain ownership of your health data at all times. See
  `PRIVACY_POLICY.md` for how it's handled.

## 4. Acceptable use

You agree to use Insina Health only for managing your own personal health
information (or that of someone you are legally authorized to manage health
information for), and not to:

- Attempt to access another person's data without authorization.
- Use the AI features for any purpose other than understanding your own
  health record.
- Attempt to circumvent the app's security, encryption, or access controls.
